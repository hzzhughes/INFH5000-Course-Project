Paper is a thin sheet that people make from wood, and almost all the paper in the world comes from trees. A large tree is cut down, and its trunk is sent to a factory. There the wood is broken into pieces and mixed with water. This soft mixture is called pulp. The pulp is spread on a moving belt, and water is pressed out of it. A hot roller dries the sheet. The sheet is pressed again until it is smooth and strong. Although one tree gives only a little paper, a forest can supply a great deal. If people plant new trees and use paper carefully, the raw material will not run out.
